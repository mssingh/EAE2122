How to use this website

Introduction

Welcome to the EAE2122 Assignment 2 website. The assignment is focussed on a severe weather event on the 26th of October 2026. You are duty forecaster on shift the evening before; you must use your knowledge from the unit, and the forecast data provided here, to develop a useful public forecast..

This page will provide some information about how to navigate the website. For details on the tasks for the assignment, see the assignment sheet on Moodle.

Part I: Forecast

This section provides forecast data as would be available on the evening of the 25th of October. Each page has maps and figures of various quantities of relevance to forecasting storms over Melbourne. Click around to explore!

Synoptic charts

Charts sowing the whole of Australia that show the synoptic situation. Are their nearby fronts, lows, jet streams?

Thunderstorm parameters

Now we zoom in to southeastern region of Australia. Forecasts for different thunderstorm related parameters are presented. When and where are the ingredients for deep convection present?

Tip: The buttons above figures allow you to change variables, so you can compare any two at once. The slider at the bottom allows you to move forward in time—but variables based on observations are available only for the past.

Part II: Verification

It is important to verify whether a forecast was good or bad after the fact. These two pages provide real data from the days in question to compare to the forecasts.

Observations

Observations at the surface, from weather stations, and in the atmosphere, from radiosonde ascents, are important for verifying the forecast stability. You can plot the sounding using the techniques we have developed in practical classes.

Radar

Radar observations help to evaluate what kind of storms and hazards may have been present.

Tip: Click the buttons at the very top of the page to hide the menu—allowing for larger figures—and to switch to dark mode.
